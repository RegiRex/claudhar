**Seventh Claudhar alpha: CPU vertex shading on four cores, no wasted driver lookups, and
a breakdown of every frame's time.** A test build, not a finished emulator.

### What changed

- **Vertices shaded on the CPU use four cores.** Until a draw's GPU vertex pipeline is
  built, its vertices are shaded on the CPU. In 0.0.6 that took 4–8 s of every 10 s on the
  emulation thread alone in Dark Moon's cold start on Qualcomm's driver (the main reason for
  its 15% speed), and in 0.0.5 0.7–1 s of every 10 s in Fire Emblem's first battles. Draws
  of 64 vertices or more are now shaded by the emulation thread and three helpers, each
  vertex once, and handed on in the draw's order, so the picture is the same. This should
  shorten the slow start, not remove it.
- **No more driver lookups that cannot find anything.** A pipeline first seen during play
  cannot be in the driver's cache, but the emulation thread asked the driver anyway: in
  Xenoblade's cold starts that took up to 0.64 s of every 10 s, with single lookups up to
  38 ms, long enough to see. Those pipelines now go straight to a background build.
- **Where each frame's time goes.** A new `Claudhar frame:` line every 10 seconds splits
  the average frame into the game's CPU work, the GPU commands it asks for (with the CPU
  vertex shading in them), presenting, and the frame limiter's spare time. It times the
  waits for the GPU, for the Vulkan worker and for a free presentation frame, and counts
  render passes and texture uploads and downloads per frame. Xenoblade at 2x stays near
  100% with no shader work going on and loses about a third at 4x; the 0.0.6 log cannot
  say why, and this line can.
- **A crash report when Android stops a game.** Dark Moon vanished 41 s into its cold
  start in 0.0.6 with no crash report: the report covered crashes and "not responding",
  not Android ending the app, for instance for low memory. Those are now reported too when
  they happen during a game. The memory line also shows the app's own memory and how much
  the device has free.
- Everything in [0.0.6-alpha](https://github.com/RegiRex/claudhar/releases/tag/0.0.6-alpha).
  Details: [Phase 1](https://github.com/RegiRex/claudhar/blob/0.0.7-alpha/docs/PHASE1_UBERSHADER.md#lookups-cpu-vertex-shading-on-several-cores-and-where-frames-go-007-alpha).

### Install

Download `claudhar-0.0.7-alpha-arm64.apk` below and install it over 0.0.6-alpha. Settings
carry over.

### What to test

- **Dark Moon from a deleted shader cache on Qualcomm's driver**, as before. In the
  `Claudhar timing:` lines, `CPU vertex shading of fallbacks` should be well under the
  4–8 s per 10 s of 0.0.6, and the speed higher while it lasts. If the game stops again,
  Claudhar should offer a crash report at its next start; please send it with the log.
- **Fire Emblem's battles at 4x**, once from a deleted cache and once with the cache kept.
  The log of a few battles shows whether the drops come with new shaders (the
  `Claudhar shading:` and `Claudhar timing:` lines) or from somewhere else (the
  `Claudhar frame:` lines).
- **Xenoblade at 2x and at 4x** in the same scenes, with the cache kept, so the
  `Claudhar frame:` lines show what grows at 4x.
- **Dark Moon on Turnip** (Per-Application Settings, Testing, GPU driver) is still worth a
  run.

### Known limits

- Not yet run on a device.
- Dark Moon's cold start on Qualcomm's driver is still expected to be slow while its
  vertex pipelines compile; the frame line will show how much of it is left.
- The limits of 0.0.3-alpha still apply.
