# Project review after 0.0.7-alpha (2026-11-10)

A review of the goals, the plan, the device logs of 0.0.2 to 0.0.6 and the code, asked for
by the owner. It says where the project stands, why the four test games behave so
differently, where the code loses time, what in the method slows the project down, and
what to do next, in order. Nothing here has been run on a device since 0.0.6; the
0.0.7 log will confirm or correct several points, and each one says which.

Sources: [the plan](PLAN.md), [Phase 0](PHASE0_FINDINGS.md) and
[Phase 1](PHASE1_UBERSHADER.md) notes, 16 game sessions in five logs (192 ten-second
intervals), and the source at `c36e4feae`.

## 1. The findings that matter most

1. **Every test so far ran with the 3D depth slider at 1% while 3D display was off.** The
   emulated 3DS reports that slider to the game whatever the display setting is
   (`hid.cpp`, `shared_page.cpp`), and a game that sees it above zero renders the top
   screen twice, once per eye. Claudhar then shows one eye and discards the other. All
   four test games support 3D. If they do render both eyes, close to half of the draws in
   every log were wasted, on the emulation thread and on the GPU. This is a setting
   (Graphics, Stereoscopy, Depth), not a Claudhar change, and it takes a minute to test.
2. **Xenoblade's cost is per draw, and it nearly doubles at 4x.** Across 63 steady
   intervals at 2x its frame took about 5 ms plus 12 to 14 µs for every draw; at 4x (seven
   intervals), about 7 ms plus 23 to 25 µs. Sonic pays about 3 µs per draw. Shader work
   plays no part in those intervals. The likeliest cause of the 4x part is render passes
   that are ended and resumed, each of which makes this GPU store and reload the whole
   picture; five to fifteen per frame would account for all of it. 0.0.7 counts them.
3. **Dark Moon has never been measured except in its first 80 seconds from an empty
   cache**, on the one driver that compiles its shaders in seconds each. Its normal speed
   in Claudhar is unknown. Turnip, which compiled Sonic's pipelines in about 30 ms, has not
   been tried on it.
4. **The plan's main rendering mode may not reach its target.** Hybrid mode shades all
   vertices on the CPU. Dark Moon's CPU-shaded draws took 0.2 to 0.45 ms each; if its
   other draws cost the same, shading all 420 to 610 per frame that way would take 110 to
   270 ms on one core, against a 16.7 ms frame (half that if finding 1 holds). 0.0.7's
   figures on four cores will say how far off it is.
5. **A New 3DS game gets an old 3DS's processor.** Azahar counts instruction time for the
   268 MHz processor. The 804 MHz mode a New 3DS title can ask for is read and stored, but
   nothing uses it for timing, and the logs do not record the game's own frame rate. A
   New 3DS exclusive such as Xenoblade can sit at 100% emulation speed and still run
   slowly in its own time.
6. **The method lacks a baseline, fixed scenes and a profile.** No log compares Claudhar
   with stock Azahar, although the rules ask for it; scenes differ from test to test; and
   where the time goes has been inferred, not measured. Each build answers about one
   question, and several of the questions above need no build at all.

## 2. Where the project stands against its goals

- **The premier rules hold.** All 204 ledger lines in the logs have `compile_waits 0` and
  `skipped 0`.
- **Phase 1's exit test is not passed.** It asks for a cold Dark Moon opening on the Thor
  with no shader hitches, and its speed against Azahar. Nothing waits for a compile, but
  the cold opening runs at 15% for as long as it has been observed, and the comparison
  with Azahar has not been made.
- **The speed target** (Dark Moon's opening at 100% at 2x on the Thor in Hybrid mode) is
  far away, and finding 4 questions the mode itself.
- **What works well:** Sonic at 4x and Fire Emblem outside battles run at about 120% to
  280% under turbo from a cold cache; leaving a game no longer freezes; per-game drivers
  and per-driver caches work.

## 3. What the logs say about each game

Steady intervals are those with no shader work (under 20 ms of CPU vertex shading, under
30 ms of lookups, under 0.5% ubershader draws), no cluster of long frames, and speed
below the turbo cap. "Draws" are per 3DS screen refresh.

| Game, driver, resolution | Steady intervals | Draws per frame | Frame time | Fit: fixed + per draw |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Sonic, Qualcomm, 2x | 10 | 480–2,040 | 10.8 ms at 1,700 | 5.2 ms + 3.3 µs |
| Sonic, Turnip, 4x | 51 | 1,240–2,090 | 12.5 ms at 1,810 | range too narrow to fit |
| Xenoblade, Qualcomm, 2x | 63 | 130–1,110 | 10.8 ms at 460 | 4.6 ms + 13.7 µs (5.9 + 11.8 above 400 draws) |
| Xenoblade, Qualcomm, 4x | 7 | 80–490 | 15.4 ms at 340 | 6.7 ms + 25.4 µs (7.9 + 22.7 without the lightest interval) |
| Fire Emblem, Qualcomm, 4x | few | about 100 | 6–7 ms (at the turbo cap) | too light to fit |
| Dark Moon, Qualcomm, 2x, cold | none | 350–700 | 100–115 ms | 58–82 ms of it CPU vertex shading |

What follows from it:

- **Sonic is fast because its draws are cheap and resolution costs it almost nothing.** It
  issues the most draws of the four and still runs at 120% to 160%. From 2x on Qualcomm's
  driver to 4x on Turnip its frame grew by about 1.7 ms.
- **Fire Emblem is light**: about a hundred draws per frame. Its battle drops in the
  0.0.5 log all came with shader work: 0.7 to 1.0 s of CPU vertex shading and about 0.1 s
  of lookups per 10 seconds, and up to 40% of draws on the ubershader, which at 4x costs
  the GPU seven to nine times a specialized shader per pixel (Phase 0). Whether battles
  still drop once their shaders are cached has not been logged.
- **Xenoblade pays four times Sonic's price per draw at 2x, and almost twice that again
  at 4x.** Since the 4x part grows with resolution, it is GPU time the emulation thread
  ends up waiting for. One ended-and-resumed render pass at 4x moves about 24 MB (store
  and reload of color and depth), which by the memory moved should take this device
  roughly 0.6 to 1.2 ms (an estimate, not a measurement); five to fifteen per frame then
  give the 11 µs per draw that 4x adds. The rest, about 8 to 10 µs per draw that does not
  depend on resolution, is on the CPU: the game's own work per object, or the renderer's
  per draw (Section 5). The logs cannot separate those; 0.0.7's frame line does.
- **Xenoblade also has clusters of 50–63 ms frames** (85 in one 10-second interval at 2x,
  45 to 66 in three at 4x), with few draws and no shader work. In the 0.0.6 run the
  renderer's GPU memory moved between 224 and 480 MiB at the same moment, which is one
  256 MiB block being allocated and freed. A video cutscene or the texture cache creating
  and destroying surfaces would both fit; the cause is not known.
- **Dark Moon is slow for one reason so far**: Qualcomm's driver takes seconds to compile
  each pipeline that holds one of its vertex shaders, and until then the CPU shades those
  vertices at 0.2 to 0.45 ms per draw. 0.0.6 emptied the queue of those pipelines three
  times faster than 0.0.5 (38 to 7 in 30 seconds) and the share of draws on specialized
  pipelines reached 72% before the process ended.

If both eyes are being rendered (finding 1), every draw count above is up to twice what
the game needs, and the per-draw costs apply to all of them.

## 4. Settings in the test configuration that cost speed

Read from the settings each session logs at its start. None is a Claudhar default.

| Setting | In every log | Android default | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Graphics, Stereoscopy, **Depth** | 1% | 0% | The game is told the 3D slider is on. With Render Stereoscopic 3D at None, the second eye is rendered and thrown away. |
| Graphics, **Accurate Multiplication** | on | off | Larger vertex shaders: more for the driver to compile (the Dark Moon problem) and more GPU work per vertex. Azahar forces it on only for Ocarina of Time 3D and three Mario & Luigi games. |
| Debug, **CPU Clock Speed** | 100% | 100% | Right for old 3DS games. A New 3DS game is not given its faster processor unless this is raised by hand (finding 5). |
| Graphics, **Skip Presenting Duplicate Frames** | off | off | Each screen refresh presents both screens again even when a 30 fps game drew nothing new. Turning it on as it stands is not a clear gain: Azahar then waits for the GPU to go idle on those refreshes. |

## 5. Code review

### 5.1 Inherited from Azahar

These are in the per-draw and per-frame paths Claudhar reuses. Sonic shows the whole path
costs about 3 µs per draw for simple draws, so none is a disaster alone; they matter for
games with heavier draws and at 4x.

| Where | What it does | Why it costs | Evidence so far |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `RenderManager::BeginRendering` | A render pass is keyed on its framebuffer **and its draw area**; a different area, a texture upload, copy, clear or download ends the pass. Every pass loads and stores color, depth and stencil. | On a tiled GPU each pass copies its targets from and to memory; at 4x that is 24 MB per pass. | Xenoblade's 4x slope. 0.0.7 counts passes and how many resume the same target. |
| `TextureRuntime::ClearTexture` | A 3DS memory fill clears a surface with a clear command outside any pass. | The cleared surface is written, then loaded again by the next pass. A clear folded into that pass's load costs neither. | By construction; two to four per frame. |
| `Surface::Download` | Each texture download ends with `scheduler.Finish()`. | The emulation thread waits until the GPU has finished everything submitted. On this driver that goes through the worker thread and a fence thread. | 0.0.7 counts downloads and GPU waits. |
| `RasterizerVulkan::TickFrame` | `scheduler.WaitWorker()` every frame. | The emulation thread waits for the Vulkan worker to record all queued commands. | 0.0.7 times it. |
| `SetupVertexArray` | Copies each draw's vertex data from guest memory to the GPU buffer, every draw, for the whole range its indices span. | Cost follows mesh size, not triangle count. No reuse between frames. | Candidate for Xenoblade's per-draw cost; not measured. |
| `UseFragmentShader`, `ShaderDiskCache::GetPipeline` | Rebuilds the fragment configuration from the registers and hashes it, and hashes the pipeline state twice, on every draw, changed or not. | A few hundred nanoseconds per draw each. | By reading. |
| `GetTextureSurface`, `GetFramebufferSurfaces` | A texture cache search for each of three texture units and both render targets on every draw. | Interval-set searches; nothing remembers the last draw's answer. | By reading. |
| `SyncTextureUnits` | A new descriptor set and three or more descriptor writes per draw. | Driver calls per draw; also the source of the "Run out of pools" lines. | By reading. |
| `RendererVulkan::SwapBuffers` | Presents both screens every refresh: a frame wait, a composite and a submit each. | Twice per refresh; half of them repeats for a 30 fps game. | 0.0.7 times presenting and the frame wait. |
| `arm_dynarmic.cpp`, `core_timing.cpp` | Guest memory through a page table rather than direct mapping; all guest cores in turn on one host thread; no 804 MHz mode. | The guest processor is the part no other work can overlap with today. | 0.0.7's "guest CPU and services". |

### 5.2 Claudhar's own code

- **CPU-vertex draws still expand every triangle.** 0.0.7 shades vertices on four threads,
  but each shaded vertex is then converted and copied three times per triangle on the
  emulation thread (`AddTriangle`, 88 bytes per corner), about six times per vertex of a
  typical mesh. If 0.0.7 gains less than about twofold, this serial tail is why. Drawing
  the shaded vertices with the game's own index buffer would remove it.
- **The ubershader's cost at 4x is not in the logs.** Phase 0 measured it at 7 to 9 times
  a specialized shader per pixel. In a cold battle at 4x a fifth to two fifths of draws
  use it. The log counts those draws but not their GPU time.
- **Background build times are no longer logged.** They were removed with Quick shader
  builds in 0.0.6, and they were what showed Qualcomm against Turnip (seconds against
  30 ms). They are needed again for the Dark Moon work.
- **Thread count.** Twelve compile threads (four each for pipelines, shader modules and
  GPU-vertex ubershader pipelines), three vertex workers, the Vulkan worker, a present
  thread per screen and the fence thread, beside the emulation thread, on eight cores of
  which three are slow. The plan's single job system is the answer; until then, the log
  should show whether the emulation thread is ever kept off its core (its CPU time
  against elapsed time).
- **Pipeline lookups that do find something still run on the emulation thread**, at 1 to
  40 ms each on Qualcomm's driver. 0.0.7 reports how many there are.
- **The parallel loop's first version hung**; a stress test written after the unit tests
  passed found it. Thread code here needs a stress test and a ThreadSanitizer run before
  it ships, as a rule.

## 6. Method

What works: the ledger proves the premier rules in every log; turbo shows headroom
without extra tooling; per-game settings and drivers make device experiments cheap;
changes are reverted when the device says they do not help (Quick shader builds).

What slows the project down, and what to do about each:

| Gap | Consequence | Change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No comparison with stock Azahar, though the rules require one | Nobody knows whether Claudhar's steady speed is better, equal or worse | One fixed scene per game, turbo on, read the speed in both, once per phase |
| Settings are logged but never checked | Depth 1% and Accurate Multiplication went unnoticed for six builds | Log one line at start naming every performance setting that is not at its default |
| Scenes differ between tests | Speeds from 85% to 377% in one game cannot be compared across builds | A fixed spot or a save state per game; later a "run this save state for 30 seconds and log a summary" entry in Testing |
| Every run starts from a deleted cache | Good for the premier rule, but steady speed is under-sampled, and Dark Moon has no steady measurement at all | Each test is two runs of the same scene: cold, then again with the cache kept |
| The game's own frame rate is not logged | Emulation speed can read 100% while the game runs slowly in its own time | Add the game's frames per interval to the timing line |
| Time is inferred from counters | Hypotheses pile up (Section 5.1 has six "by reading") | A sampling profiler of the emulation thread, as a Testing switch: a timer signal records where the thread is about a thousand times a second and the log lists the top functions at exit. The library keeps its symbols, so no computer is needed |
| Whether turbo was on is not logged | A speed near 100% is ambiguous | Already answered by 0.0.7's frame limiter time; also log the cap |
| Thermal and clock state unknown | A hot or power-saving device looks like a slow build | Log the emulation thread's core, that core's frequency and Android's thermal status each interval |
| A build per question | A week per answer | Settings and driver experiments first (Section 8, tier 0); they need no build |
| No end-to-end test of the renderer | The plan's "tests replay recorded GPU traces" does not exist; renderer changes are first tested on the owner's device | Record a few frames of GPU commands on the device (Azahar has the recorder; it needs a Testing switch) and write a small player that replays them on a computer. Traces contain game data and stay private, like logs |

## 7. The plan

- **Hybrid mode needs its premise checked before Phase 4 is built on it** (finding 4).
  0.0.7 logs the vertices shaded on the CPU and the time on four threads; that gives a
  cost per vertex and from it whether any number of cores reaches 16.7 ms for Dark Moon.
  If not, the route to full speed for such games is GPU vertex shading with something
  that needs no compile per shader while the real one builds.
- **Qualcomm's compile times are not a Thor curiosity.** The Duo, the minimum device, has
  no Turnip. A vertex shader interpreter on the GPU (one compile, kept in the ubershader
  cache) is the candidate; the probe tool should measure its compile time and per-vertex
  cost on the device before it is built, as Phase 0 did for the fragment ubershader.
- **Render-pass work may need to come forward from Phase 4** if 0.0.7 shows many passes
  per frame: folding clears into pass loads, keeping uploads out of passes, and not
  ending a pass for a different draw area. These fit Azahar's renderer.
- **New 3DS games need two things the plan puts last or not at all**: the 804 MHz mode,
  and guest cores on their own threads (Phase 8). For Xenoblade these may matter more than
  the GPU thread.
- **The GPU command thread (Phases 2–3) remains the largest general gain.** For Sonic
  about 3 µs of each draw's cost would move off the emulation thread.

## 8. What to do next, in order

**Tier 0: on the device, no build needed.**

1. Set Depth to 0% and replay any scene. If the draws per frame in the log fall by up to
   half, finding 1 is confirmed, and every speed measured so far was held back by it.
2. Dark Moon on Turnip, from a deleted cache.
3. Dark Moon with Accurate Multiplication off (and note any change in the picture).
4. Xenoblade at 4x on Turnip, same scene as on Qualcomm's driver.
5. Xenoblade at 1x against 2x in one scene: no difference means 2x is limited by the
   CPU side; a clear difference means the GPU side.
6. Xenoblade with CPU Clock Speed (Debug) at 200% and 300%, with the performance overlay
   showing both FPS, which is the game's own frame rate, and Speed.
7. One scene per game on stock Azahar with the same settings, turbo on.

**Tier 1: the next build, small and certain.**

1. Report the 3D slider as zero when no display shows 3D, so Depth cannot cost speed by
   accident. This is what a real 3DS does with its slider down.
2. Log lines: settings not at default; the game's frame rate; background builds (count,
   average and slowest time per kind); the emulation thread's CPU time, core and
   frequency; thermal status.
3. The profiler switch.

**Tier 2: once 0.0.7's frame line is in, whichever it points at.**

- Many render passes: fold clears, keep uploads out of passes, key passes on the
  framebuffer alone where the driver allows.
- Long worker or GPU waits: remove the per-frame worker wait; avoid the full GPU wait on
  downloads where the data is not needed at once.
- High "GPU commands" with few passes: remember the last draw's fragment configuration,
  textures and render targets while nothing changed; measure vertex copies.
- High "presenting": skip repeated presents without waiting for the GPU.
- CPU vertex shading still slow: indexed CPU-vertex draws.

**Tier 3: the plan's phases, with the order checked against the data.**

- GPU command thread (Phases 2–3).
- New 3DS clock mode and guest cores on threads, if Xenoblade's guest share is large.
- Vertex shader interpreter, after a probe measurement.
- Hybrid mode only if the vertex cost supports it.

## 9. What would change these conclusions

- If Depth 0% does not lower the draw counts, the games ignore the slider and finding 1 is
  wrong; the rest stands.
- If 0.0.7 shows few render passes for Xenoblade at 4x, its 4x cost is fill rate or
  downloads, and the remedy is different.
- If Dark Moon is fast on Turnip from a cold cache, its problem is the driver's compiler
  alone, and the Thor has an answer today while the Duo still needs one.
